# IB Physics HL — Definitions

A TI-84 Plus CE reference: the IB definitions you need to know, in words. The test pages add the formulas the data booklet does not give, with a note at the top of each page listing the ones it does. All the text lives in `src/content.c`.

## Test unit: magnetic fields, motion in fields and induction

The left column on the home screen covers the 2026 test (D.3 and D.4), point by point from the study guide:

| Tile | Covers |
|---|---|
| Magnetic fields | bar magnet, Oersted, fields of a wire, solenoid and coil, right-hand rules, two wires, flux density |
| Magnetic forces | force on a moving charge and on a wire, left-hand rule, motor effect, parallel wires, definition of the ampere |
| Charges in fields | accelerating through a p.d., electronvolt, parabolic, circular and helical paths, charge-to-mass ratio, velocity selector, Bainbridge mass spectrometer |
| Flux & Faraday | flux, flux linkage, Faraday's and Lenz's laws, ways to induce an EMF, the EMF of a moving rod, self-induction |
| AC generator | sinusoidal EMF, effect of rotation frequency, 90 degree phase difference between flux and EMF, increasing output |
| Derivations | step by step from booklet formulas: radius, period and helix in a magnetic field, charge-to-mass ratio, velocity selector, spectrometer, force between wires, the EMF of a moving rod three ways, coil entering a field, rotating coil |

**Sketches** describes every field pattern, particle path and generator graph you have to draw. **Maxi problem** goes through a rectangular coil moving into, through and out of a uniform field, with both graphs.

## Every other IB theme

The right column: A Motion & energy, B Matter & heat, C Wave behaviour, D Fields, E Atoms & nuclei, plus Tools & tips (uncertainties and how to word a definition). HL-only topics are marked (HL).

The previous test unit (electrostatics and electric currents) is condensed into Matter & heat (current and circuits) and Fields (electric fields), keeping the teacher's exact wording for electric field strength, electric potential, potential difference, Ohm's law and EMF.

## Mix-ups

Pairs of terms that are easy to confuse, compared side by side: magnetism pairs such as the left-hand rule vs the right-hand grip rule, induction pairs such as Faraday vs Lenz, and the pairs from earlier units.
